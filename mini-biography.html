Mini-Biography of APJ Abdul Kalam

General Information

A. P. J. Abdul Kalam was one of India's most respected scientists and leaders. Known as the Missile Man of India.

He played a key role in the country's space and missile programs and later served as the 11th President of India.

His dedication, humility, and passion for inspiring young people made him a beloved role model across the world.

Achievements

Played a key role in developing India's missile programme.

Earned the title "Missile Man of India."

Contributed to the success of the Pokhran-II nuclear tests (1998).

Served as the 11th President of India (2002–2007).

Was widely known as the "People's President."

A.P.J. Abdul Kalam won the Nobel Prize in Physics

Received India's highest civilian award, the Bharat Ratna.

Also received the Padma Vibhushan and Padma Bhushan.

Authored inspiring books such as Wings of Fire, Ignited Minds, and India 2020.

Inspired millions of students through his speeches, books, and dedication to education.

Left a lasting legacy in science, technology, and nation-building.

Early Life of A.P.J. Abdul Kalam

Born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, India.

Full name: Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam.

Belonged to a humble middle-class family.

His father, Jainulabdeen, was a boat owner and imam, while his mother, Ashiamma, was a kind and caring homemaker.

Grew up with four brothers and one sister.

From a young age, he learned the values of hard work, honesty, discipline, and simplicity.

To help support his family, he sold newspapers before and after school.

Was an intelligent and curious student who loved science, mathematics, and learning new things.

His teachers recognized his talent and encouraged him to dream big.

Completed his schooling in Ramanathapuram.

Studied Physics at St. Joseph's College, Tiruchirappalli, graduating in 1954.

Later earned a degree in Aeronautical Engineering from the Madras Institute of Technology (MIT).

Despite facing financial difficulties, he worked hard and remained determined to achieve his goals.

His early experiences shaped his character and inspired him to dedicate his life to science, education, and serving the nation.
